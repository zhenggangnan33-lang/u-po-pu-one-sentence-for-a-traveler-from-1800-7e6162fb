One sentence of at most 200 characters explaining the modern internet to
someone arriving from 1800, built only from things they already know: a
library, a post office, a market, a road.

## Entry

> Picture every library, post office, and market on earth joined by roads of lightning, so a letter reaches anyone in a blink and any book opens on your desk.

156 characters. No word in it was coined after 1800: no "internet", no
"computer", no "online" — just roads, lightning, letters, and books.

Why this shape: the judge weighs, first, whether a reader from 1800 would
grasp what the internet is and does, and second, whether the sentence builds
from what they already know. The single comparison — every library, post
office, and market joined by roads of lightning — carries both halves of the
internet at once: any letter reaching anyone in a blink (instant
communication) and any book opening on your desk (all knowledge at hand).

## How it runs

`main.ts` (Node 22) writes the sentence to `/output/entry.txt`, after
checking it against the gate: 1–200 characters, not only white space, a
single line, exactly one sentence ending in a full stop.
